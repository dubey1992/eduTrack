Many schools still keep staff attendance on paper: a register by the office, signed on the way in. It works, more or less, but it is slow at the busiest moment of the morning, easy to fill in later, and hard to total up at the end of the month when payroll needs it.

Checking in from a phone solves most of that. It also raises fair questions from staff about tracking and privacy. A system that answers those questions well is one people will actually use.

## What a good check-in has to do

A check-in should answer one question honestly: was this person at school, and when did they arrive? To do that it needs three things.

- **It happens at school.** A check-in from home, or from the bus stop down the road, should not count.
- **It records the time.** The arrival time is what tells the office who was late, without anyone keeping a separate list.
- **It fits the rest of the day.** Approved leave, holidays and half days should already be on the register, so nobody is marked absent for a day off they were given.

## Use location, but only at the moment of check-in

The simplest way to know a check-in happened at school is the phone's location. The school marks where it is once, sets a radius around it, and a check-in only counts inside it.

How that location is used matters a great deal to staff.

### Ask only when they tap

The phone's location should be read once, at the moment a person checks in or out, and never in between. Nothing should follow anybody around during the day, after school or at the weekend. Say so plainly when you introduce it: it removes most of the worry before it starts.

### Refuse a fake or a guess

Some phones can pretend to be somewhere else, and a weak signal can give a position hundreds of metres out. A check-in from a faked location, or one whose accuracy is too poor to trust, should be refused with a clear message, rather than quietly accepted or quietly marked absent.

## Be fair about the edge cases

Most disputes come from the ordinary exceptions, so decide them in advance.

- **A forgotten check-out.** People forget. Filling it in with the school's closing time, and flagging it for the office to look at, is fairer than marking a half day.
- **Late arrivals.** Work lateness out from the check-in time against the start of the first period, so it is the same rule for everyone and nobody has to argue about it.
- **Leave.** A day of approved leave belongs on the register automatically, and a check-in on that day should not be possible. If someone has applied for leave, it is worth settling the application first.
- **Holidays.** A school that is closed has nothing to check in to.

## Let it flow into the rest of the month

The real payoff comes later. When check-ins, leave and holidays are all in one register, the month's attendance is ready the moment payroll needs it, including half days and unpaid absences. Nobody has to retype a paper register into a spreadsheet on the last working day.
